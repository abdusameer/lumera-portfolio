How a call goes

The questions change by business. The sequence doesn’t.

  1. 1. Answer the call

    A consistent greeting whenever the line is set to answer.

  2. 2. Understand the request

    An appointment, a repair, a table or an order.

  3. 3. Collect the details you need

    Only the questions your business asks before a visit or a call back.

  4. 4. Check your calendar, where one is connected

    Real openings come from a connected calendar or booking tool. Without one, the receptionist takes a request and your team confirms it.

  5. 5. Confirm the next step

    The caller hears what was recorded and what happens next.

  6. 6. Pass it to your team

    An organized summary instead of a voicemail.

Set up around your business

The pattern stays the same. These parts are configured for you, and you approve them.

  • Services

    What you offer, how long each takes and who can do it.

  • Hours and scheduling rules

    When the line answers, buffers between visits, service areas and how far ahead people can book.

  • Questions

    The details your team needs before a visit or a call back.

  • Urgent and unusual calls

    When a person should take over and how they’re reached, under rules you approve. A live transfer during the call isn’t part of the current setup.

Missed-call text-back

For calls nobody can pick up.

  • A text, not silence

    When a call is missed, the caller gets a text back, so they’re not left wondering whether anyone will call.

Questions

What happens when the receptionist can’t help a caller?

It collects the caller's details and passes an organized summary to your team, so a person can follow up. Which calls count as urgent, and how they reach your staff, follow rules you approve during setup.

A live transfer to a person during the call isn't part of the current setup.

Do I need to be technical?

No technical experience required. We set things up and explain them in plain language.
